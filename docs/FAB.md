# Features, Advantages, Benefits

Visitor-facing FAB. **Feature** is what it does. **Advantage** is why that is better than the usual workaround. **Benefit** is what the organizer or player feels.

Engineering and POC internals stay in `RELATED-POCS.md`.

---

## Hub — `PlanFunGames.com`

The hub talks about **where the group already lives**, not about a sport.

### 1. Stay in the group chat you already have

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PlanFunGames attaches to GroupMe first, instead of replacing the chat your group already opens. | Nobody has to move the social graph into a new app. | The night still starts in the chat people already open. |

### 2. Players do not install anything

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only the organizer uses the extra tools. Players keep chat + court. | Rec groups will not adopt a product that asks twenty people to create accounts. | You can try it on Tuesday without a software rollout. |

### 3. Voice remote for the organizer

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A voice-centric phone app lets the organizer capture scores and hear what’s next without typing in bright sun. | Faster and safer than unlocking GroupMe between points. | Games turn over without the organizer hunched over the phone. |

### 4. One night, one source of truth

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chat, voice, and assignments all describe the same rec night. | The group does not fork into what an app says vs who’s actually in. | People trust the assignments because they match the I’m-in list they already used. |

## Pickleball spoke — `pickleball.PlanFunGames.com`

Speak pickleball: rec nights, I’m-in lists, sit-outs, mixing. Do not lead with whiteboard-photo internals. Never “open play.”

### 1. Keep the rec night social

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The group still gathers in GroupMe and on the courts. | No “download our app or you don’t play.” | The night still feels like pickleball, not like onboarding. |

### 2. Fairer games without a tournament desk

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The organizer gets help with who plays whom, who sits, and how the next round should look. | Beats stacking the same four and stranding the 3.0s. | More games that feel right; fewer “I sat twice and they played together again.” |

### 3. Sit-outs that do not start an argument

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rotation and sit planning are part of running the night, not a hallway debate. | Odd counts and extra bodies are normal in rec pickleball. | People know when they are up, and the organizer is not the villain. |

### 4. Talk the scores in, don’t type them

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voice capture for court scores between games. | Typing `court 3, 11-7` with sweaty hands is the live pain. | The next round starts while people are still catching their breath. |

## Tennis spoke — `tennis.PlanFunGames.com`

Thinner than pickleball. Tennis language: courts, round robins, ladder nights, who is sitting, club chat.

### 1. Run the round robin from the club chat

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The club or ladder already lives in a group thread. PlanFunGames attaches there. | No second roster in a tennis-club app nobody opens. | Tonight’s courts get filled from the same chat used to say “I’m in.” |

### 2. Who sits, who plays, which court

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Help rotating bodies across a limited court list. | Tennis nights bottleneck on court count, not on “did we mix DUPR.” | Fewer people lingering, fewer unused courts, less organizer math. |

### 3. Organizer voice, players just show up

| Feature | Advantage | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The organizer can speak results and hear the next pairing. | Ladder nights move faster when the desk is a phone, not a clipboard. | Members play tennis; one person is not stuck as the human draw sheet. |
